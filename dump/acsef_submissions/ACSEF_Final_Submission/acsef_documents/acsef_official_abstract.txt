Neonatal sepsis diagnosis is difficult because transcriptomic signals vary across cohorts, laboratory protocols, and batch effects. We investigated whether a biologically constrained graph architecture could improve robust classification and produce interpretable biomarkers. We integrated public GEO whole-blood cohorts (GSE25504 and GSE69686) for model development and used GSE26440 as an external validation cohort. Expression data were harmonized by gene mapping, filtering, normalization, and ComBat batch correction. We built a multiplex hypergraph model with three biological relations (KEGG pathway co-membership, STRING protein interaction, and co-expression), then applied relation attention, gene scoring, an MLP classifier, and a domain-adversarial head to reduce batch leakage. Five-fold cross-validation on the main sepsis model produced AUROC 0.9796, accuracy 0.9779, and F1 0.9733. External validation on GSE26440 (n=104) preserved performance (AUROC 0.9856, accuracy 0.9519, F1 0.9697), indicating generalization beyond training cohorts. Explainable AI with custom Integrated Gradients identified high-impact biomarkers including TNFAIP6, S100A12, RETN, and CD52, and generated ranked attributions for 100 genes. A no-MLP ablation collapsed toward random chance (reported AUROC about 0.4 to 0.5), showing that non-linear feature integration was essential. The same design principles also transferred to osteogenesis imperfecta analysis, supporting broader rare-disease applicability. These results suggest graph-guided, domain-aware models can improve both predictive reliability and biological interpretability in translational genomics.
