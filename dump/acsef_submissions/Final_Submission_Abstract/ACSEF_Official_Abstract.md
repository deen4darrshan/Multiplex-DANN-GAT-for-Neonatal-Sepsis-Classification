**Project Title:** Multiplex Hypergraph Deep Learning for Explainable Neonatal Sepsis Diagnosis

---

Neonatal sepsis is a leading cause of newborn mortality, but gold-standard blood cultures take 24–48 hours, forcing empirical antibiotic use and fueling antimicrobial resistance. Transcriptomic machine-learning classifiers offer rapid diagnosis but suffer from batch effects across cohorts and lack biological interpretability. We developed a hybrid Multiplex Hypergraph Convolutional Network + MLP (HGCN-MLP) with domain-adversarial training to achieve robust, explainable disease classification. The model constructs a multiplex hypergraph combining KEGG pathways, STRING protein interactions, and fold-specific co-expression, then applies relation attention, a gene-scoring mask, and an MLP classifier with gradient-reversal to suppress batch confounding. Using harmonized GEO cohorts (N = 319 neonates), internal cross-validation yielded AUROC 0.980 and accuracy 97.8%, outperforming the best baseline (AUROC 0.913). External validation on an independent pediatric cohort (N = 104) achieved AUROC 0.986 and F1 0.970. A pure-HGCN ablation collapsed to chance, confirming non-linear MLP integration is critical. Explainability analysis identified biologically plausible biomarkers including TNFAIP6, S100A12, RETN, and CD52. Demonstrating scalability, the architecture transferred to Alzheimer's disease (N=222; AUROC 0.944 vs. 0.828 baseline) and osteogenesis imperfecta (N=34; 0.774 vs. 0.503). Across three diseases, the framework averaged AUROC 0.899 against 0.748 for baselines, establishing a generalizable, explainable AI approach for translational genomics.
