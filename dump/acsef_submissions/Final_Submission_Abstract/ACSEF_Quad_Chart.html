Multiplex Hypergraph Deep Learning for Explainable Neonatal Sepsis Diagnosis
HS-BCOM-390-T3
Q1: Engineering Problem & Objectives
  • Neonatal sepsis is a leading cause of newborn mortality, but gold-standard blood cultures take 24–48 hours.
  • Early empirical antibiotics fuel antimicrobial resistance.
  • Previous models on high-dimensional gene expression suffer from batch effects across cohorts.
  • Objective: Engineer a robust, explainable graph neural network (GNN) for transcriptomic classification without shortcut learning.
Sepsis cohort data pipeline policy flow
Q2. Project Design
Gene interaction network structural prior
  • Harmonized GSE25504 and GSE69686 databases (N=319) preserving biological signal using ComBat.
  • Constructed a 3-relation multiplex hypergraph (KEGG, STRING, Co-expression) over top 2,000 varying genes.
  • Implemented a hybrid HGCN-MLP with learned relation attention and a gene-scoring mask.
  • Applied Domain-Adversarial (DANN) training to suppress cohort confounding.
Q3: Data Analysis & Results
  • Internal 5-fold CV achieved AUROC 0.980, F1 0.973 on neonatal data.
  • External validation (GSE26440, N=104) confirmed generalizability with AUROC 0.986.
  • Outperformed logistic regression baselines (AUROC 0.913 internally).
  • Pure-GNN ablation collapsed to chance, proving non-linear MLP integration is critical.
Model ROC curve performance vs. baselines
Q4: Interpretation & Conclusions
  • Feature attribution identified top genes (TNFAIP6, S100A12) with established innate immune roles.
  • The framework successfully scaled to Alzheimer's Disease (N=222; AUROC 0.944).
  • Generalized flawlessly to Osteogenesis Imperfecta predicting accurately over baselines.
  • Conclusion: Designed an explainable, scalable system for translational genomics bridging cohort gaps.
